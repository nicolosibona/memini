# What stood out at the Shanghai Impact Hackathon

Memini placed first in the China business track at the Shanghai Impact Hackathon on 21 November 2026. The two public organizer recaps are useful evidence for how the demo was understood.

## What the organizers highlighted

- PROPELLER opened with “Hardware is hard; cross-border hardware is harder.” Its write-up praised the engine for catching engineering and component mismatches “before you ever wire money to a factory.” This is the clearest language for the buyer’s decision: find discrepancies while there is still time to act on them. [PROPELLER’s winners announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/globalpropeller_thearch-theimpactroom-crossborder-activity-7530179498617671680-JusU)
- The Impact Room called Memini “A WeChat assistant for companies sourcing electronics from China” and singled out rules that “mark each parameter pass, fail or missing.” The recap also highlighted the comparison in both languages and the practical handoff: a bilingual spreadsheet and a reply for the supplier. [The Impact Room’s event recap](https://theimpactroom.co/sih-01/)

## Suggested wording for the next demo

**Catch supplier spec mismatches before you pay.**

On WeChat, compare your requirements with the supplier’s documents and send back a bilingual checklist of what matches, what fails, and what’s missing.

Show an actual mismatch in the first few seconds. The hackathon demo used a supplier value of 150 mV against a limit of 100 mV, then showed the bilingual spreadsheet and the reply ready to forward. [Memini pitch deck, slides 9–10](memini-pitch-deck.pdf)

The award and recaps document organizer recognition. Buyer interviews and pilots can test whether the pre-payment framing also drives interest from importers.
